Google Sheets vs Excel for Budgeting: Which Should You Use?

Google Sheets or Excel for your budget? A plain comparison of cost, phone use, offline access, privacy and formulas, and how to choose in one minute.

The short version

  • Google Sheets is free, works on any device and saves itself, which suits most small-income budgets.
  • Excel works fully offline and can keep your file on your own computer. Excel for the web is free.
  • Budget templates in .xlsx format work in both, so you can switch any time.

You've decided to budget in a spreadsheet. Good choice: it's free, private and flexible. Now the next question: Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel?

For most people with a small income, the short answer is Google Sheets. It's free, it works on every device, and it saves itself. But Excel has real advantages, and if you already have it, there's no need to switch. Here's how they compare for budgeting.

The quick comparison

Why Google Sheets suits most budgets

It's free with no catch. All you need is a Google account, which many people already have through Gmail or an Android phone.

It works everywhere. The same budget opens on your laptop, a library computer and your phone. Logging a purchase in the Sheets app at the till takes a few seconds, which is the habit that makes tracking expenses stick.

It saves itself. There's no "Save" button to forget and no file to lose when a laptop dies. Google keeps a version history, so you can go back if you break a formula.

Sharing is simple. If you budget with a partner or a housemate, you can both edit the same sheet at the same time.

Where Excel is better

It works fully offline. The desktop app doesn't need an internet connection at all. Google Sheets can work offline too, but only in Chrome or Edge, with the Google Docs Offline extension, and only if you turned on offline access while you were online.

Your file can stay on your computer. If you prefer your financial data not to be in the cloud at all, the desktop app lets you keep it as a file on your own device.

It handles big and complex workbooks. For years of transactions, pivot tables and charts, the desktop app is faster and has more tools. A personal budget rarely needs this, but some people enjoy building detailed models.

You may already have it. Many workplaces, schools and universities give students and staff Microsoft 365. If you have it, use it.

The free version of Excel

You don't have to pay for Excel to budget in it. With a free Microsoft account, you can use Excel for the web in a browser. Microsoft also includes 5 GB of free OneDrive storage, which is far more than any budget needs. The catch is that files must be saved in and shared from OneDrive, and some advanced features are kept for paid Microsoft 365 plans. For a budget, the free version is enough.

Do budget templates work in both?

Yes, if they stick to common functions. Everything a budget usually needs, like SUM, SUMIFS, IF, COUNTIFS, EDATE and dropdown lists, works the same way in both. Google Sheets opens and edits Excel's .xlsx files, and you can download any Google Sheet as an .xlsx file.

A few things don't carry over:

  • Macros. Excel's VBA macros don't run in Google Sheets. Google uses its own scripting language instead.
  • Some newer functions. A few functions only exist in one of the two, or only in newer versions of Excel. For example, MINIFS needs Excel 2019 or newer.
  • Some formatting. Fancy charts and colours can look slightly different after moving between them.

How to choose in one minute

Choose Google Sheets if:

  • you don't already have Excel
  • you want to use your budget on your phone
  • you budget with someone else
  • you'd rather not think about saving files

Choose Excel if:

  • you already have Microsoft 365 through work, school or home
  • you often have no internet connection
  • you want your financial data stored only on your own computer
  • you enjoy building bigger, more detailed spreadsheets

If you're still not sure, start in Google Sheets. It costs nothing, and because both use the same file format, you can move to Excel any time and take your budget with you.

What about budgeting apps?

Apps can import your bank transactions automatically, which saves typing. But many good ones charge a subscription, only work with banks in some countries, or ask for access to your bank login. A spreadsheet is free and private, and it makes you look at every number, which is often the point. Our comparison of free budgeting apps looks at what each one really offers.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Sheets really free? Yes, for personal use with a Google account. Your sheets count towards the free storage in your Google account, and a budget takes up almost none of it.

Is my financial data safe in Google Sheets? Your sheet is private unless you share it. Protect your Google account with a strong password and two-step verification, since anyone who gets into the account can see your files.

Can I move my budget from Excel to Google Sheets? Yes. Upload the .xlsx file to Google Drive and open it. To turn it into a normal Google Sheet, choose File, then Save as Google Sheets.

Can I move from Google Sheets to Excel? Yes. In Google Sheets, choose File, then Download, then Microsoft Excel (.xlsx).

Which is easier for a beginner? They are very similar. Google Sheets has fewer menus, which some beginners find less overwhelming. If you learn SUMIFS in one, it works the same in the other: our SUMIF and SUMIFS guide has examples to practise on.
