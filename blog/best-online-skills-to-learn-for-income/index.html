Best Online Skills to Learn to Make Money in 2027

Eight online skills to learn to make money in 2027, with what each involves, free ways to start, how long the basics take, and a 90-day plan.

The short version

  • Choose a skill by three tests: people pay for it now, you can learn the basics free, and you can show proof of work without a degree.
  • Employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030, with AI and data skills growing fastest, says the World Economic Forum.
  • One skill taken to a paid level beats five started. Give one of them 90 days.

There is no shortage of lists telling you which skill will make you rich. This isn't one of them.

These are eight skills that businesses pay freelancers and remote workers for today, that you can start learning without spending money, and that should still be useful through 2027. What each pays depends on your country, your niche and how good you get, so there are no income figures here. Check current job boards and freelance platforms for real rates before you commit.

Why skills matter more than ever

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 surveyed employers around the world. They expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030. The fastest-growing skills on their list are AI and big data, followed by networks and cybersecurity, then technological literacy.

Tech isn't the whole story, though. The same report names analytical thinking as the top core skill employers want right now, with seven in ten companies calling it essential.

Put those together and the message is clear: learn a practical digital skill, learn the AI tools that go with it, and keep the human parts (judgement, accuracy, taste) sharp. That combination is what clients pay for.

How these skills were chosen

Each one passes three tests:

  1. Demand you can see. Open any freelance marketplace or remote job board and there are paid listings for it today.
  2. Free to start. Good learning material exists at no cost, and the basic tools are free.
  3. Proof beats credentials. Clients ask to see work, not certificates.

1. Writing and copywriting

Articles, product descriptions, emails, website copy. Businesses always need words, and they need a person to make sure those words are correct, on-brand and worth reading.

  • Start free: write five sample pieces in one niche you understand and publish them somewhere you can share a link.
  • Basics take: 1 to 3 months of regular practice.
  • First client: small businesses and blogs in your niche.

2. Video editing

Short-form video is how most businesses and creators reach people now, and editing is the bottleneck.

  • Start free: free editors such as CapCut and DaVinci Resolve cover everything a beginner needs.
  • Basics take: 2 to 3 months.
  • First client: a creator or local business already posting unedited or badly edited video.

3. Spreadsheets and data analysis

Cleaning data, building reports and answering business questions with numbers. This is the "AI and big data" trend at its most accessible. Strong Excel or Google Sheets skills are useful in almost every office job, and SQL extends them.

  • Start free: practise on public datasets. Rebuild a report you find online.
  • Basics take: 2 to 4 months for confident spreadsheet work.
  • First client: small businesses drowning in exports they can't read.

4. Web development basics

Building and fixing websites with HTML, CSS and JavaScript. AI assistants write a lot of code now. Someone still has to know whether it is right, put it online and maintain it.

  • Start free: freeCodeCamp and The Odin Project are complete, free curricula.
  • Basics take: 4 to 8 months to build simple sites on your own.
  • First client: a local business with no site, or a broken one.

5. Graphic and social media design

Posts, thumbnails, presentations, simple brand kits. Most small businesses need a steady stream and have nobody to make it.

  • Start free: Canva for speed, Figma for more control. Both have free plans.
  • Basics take: 1 to 3 months.
  • First client: student societies, tutors, cafes, online sellers.

6. SEO and content marketing

Helping websites get found on search engines: researching what people search for, improving pages and planning content.

  • Start free: Google's own Search Central documentation, then practise on your own blog, where you can watch the results.
  • Basics take: 3 to 6 months, because results are slow to show.
  • First client: a small site that publishes regularly and gets little traffic.

7. Bookkeeping

Recording income and expenses, reconciling accounts and preparing figures for an accountant. It is unglamorous and steady, and every business needs it.

  • Start free: the free training offered by accounting software companies, plus basic accounting courses on open learning sites.
  • Basics take: 3 to 6 months. Rules are country-specific, so learn your local ones.
  • First client: freelancers and sole traders who hate doing their own.

8. AI-assisted workflows and automation

Setting up tools so repetitive tasks happen with less manual work: sorting emails, drafting replies, moving data between apps. Many businesses know they want this and don't know where to begin.

  • Start free: automate three of your own repetitive tasks using the free tiers of AI assistants and automation tools.
  • Basics take: 1 to 3 months, faster if you already have another skill on this list.
  • First client: a business owner who complains about a task they do every single day.

How to pick one skill

  • What do you already do for fun or for friends? That is usually the fastest route.
  • How soon do you need income? Writing, design and video reach paid work quickest.
  • Do you prefer people or screens? Bookkeeping and data are quiet work. Social media and SEO involve more client contact.

A 90-day plan that works for any of them

  1. Days 1 to 30: learn the basics from one free course. Do the exercises. Don't only watch.
  2. Days 31 to 60: build three small projects that look like real client work.
  3. Days 61 to 90: get one paying client at a modest price, and deliver well.

Here is how that might look. Amira, a made-up example, picks design. In month one she finishes a free Canva course and copies ten posts she admires to learn layout. In month two she designs a month of posts for an imaginary cafe, a tutor and a gym. In month three she shows those to five local businesses, and a tutoring centre pays her a small fee for four posts a week.

It isn't much money yet. It is a paying client, a real portfolio piece and a reference, and those three things make the second client far easier than the first.

For what the early months of client work usually feel like, see your first 90 days of freelancing. If you are a student, several of these zero-cost side hustles let you practise while being paid.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying an expensive course first. Start free. Pay later, once you know exactly what you are missing.
  • Collecting certificates. A link to work you made is worth more.
  • Switching skills every few weeks. The early stage is dull for all of them. Push through one.
  • Waiting until you feel ready. Take a small job slightly before you do.

When the first payments arrive, give them a job before they vanish. A simple budget is enough.
